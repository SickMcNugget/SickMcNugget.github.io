Microsoft are stuck in an eternal loop of hiring and firing employees. When you hire people, it appears as though your company is growing, which is a great look for stock prices. When you fire people, it shows that you are focusing on quality over quantity, removing employees that aren't as effective so that you can focus on your greater vision. The effect this has on stock value is... just as good as hiring en masse (I don't get it either).

This article is a thank you to the transient employees of Microsoft, saddled with the task of creating icons infected with smallpox for the purpose of making an empty <div> seem less frightening.

## The good
Let's start with the 'good' icons. The ones that closely follow Microsoft's current, soulless branding. For some reason these icons are filled with gradients and light sources that originate from multiple points. There's an odd matte coating on everything, and some of the edges don't line up properly. My coworker's assumption is that Microsoft are generating these icons with AI and measuring how well they do (whatever the f*** that means). I like to think that there's some people working on these images, soullessly drawing rounded rectangles for an entire work day.

## The bad
The **dots** we saw in the 'empty folder' icons seem to be an ailment that Microsoft suffered from for a while. If these images *were* generated by a neural network, then that neural network had digital leprosy, because the images above are the updated, "latest and greatest" versions of the outlook icons. Let's go through the icons that: a. contracted smallpox and b. don't fit anywhere within Microsoft's branding.

Let's start with the envelopes. Here we begin the spread of contagion. These icons are the reason I'm making this blog post. Whoever began the pandemic must have sneezed on their priority mail, infecting one of the magnificent 7, attempting to bring down an empire.
