I've been looking into Zachtronics recently, since for about the past month I've been trying to learn how to develop games. I'm using Unity for most of my learning at the moment, but there may be a time in the future where I don't tie myself to a big general purpose beast as Unity is. Zach Barth, the Zach behind the name, is quite the passionate fellow, and I'm inclined to agree with many of his takes on productivity and the creation process in general. A great archive of his early work is shown in [this](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Df9pz_EmKhA) talk that Zach did at Google.

I'm also interested in exploring [ZACH-LIKE](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1098840/ZACHLIKE/), where Zach shares some of his behind the scenes design documents that he creates for his games. Surely a resource like this is extremely uncommon, and should be quite valuable as to what the process of design and iteration actually looks like in practice from someone that is quite successful in the industry.

Some other cool things I've found as a result of this search are:
- Matthew Seiji's book [**Process**](https://matthewseiji.com/process). Matthew Seiji is/was the writer of Zachtronics.
- Jonathan Stroh's [art](https://jonathanstroh.com/). Jonathan is/was the artist of Zachtronics.

Particularly, I really like Stroh's art. I've started playing Zachtronics games, beginning with Opus Magnum, and I was fairly struct by the art of the world in that game. There doesn't appear to be any animation, but the music and the highly detailed still imagery still provides ample atmosphere for the story.

Reading through an old [AMA](https://www.reddit.com/r/IAmA/comments/994t8l/im_zach_barth_the_creative_director_of_the_game/) that Zach did on Reddit, I found one Question/Answer that I feel is quite powerful for productivity. I think it myself quite often, but I'm sorely lacking in my ability to focus on just getting the thing done. I'm planning to be that person, though.

> dionvc: When you were programming on your own all that time ago how did you get the motivation to finish a game? I start work on a game but I quickly lose focus.
> 
> Zach: I became obsessed with scope. The only way I could finish a game was to set the scope small enough that I'd finish before I got bored. Knowing which corners to cut is an important life skill if you want to be someone who gets shit done.

Anyway, this is all a bit rambly, since I just want to use this as a form of long-term memory.
