I'm definitely getting way ahead of myself, since I still haven't completed a single video game, but I've been looking into game distribution today. I mean the nitty gritty details of physical distribution (but I'd also be interested in the details of digital distribution, too). The kind of things that interest me are: boxes for shipping, custom CD covers, custom CD art, custom art on the boxes and logistics.

For example, what did it cost to print and distribute PAL copies of Crash Bandicoot to retailers in Australia back in 1996? Also, what's the process for making those gorgeous, glossy game manuals that used to come packaged with games? We still have our copy of Carnage Heart for PS1, where you would place chips in a circuit to control the functionality of a Mech that would battle against other mechs. I can picture the odd purplish hue of the cover with a mech surrounded by fire even now.

Getting back on track, I've found a pretty good Reddit post that has some useful (although admittedly American) resources for physical distribution of a CD-based game. Since I don't trust the internet to archive anything anymore (other than what I put on it lol), I'm going to paste **felep22**'s question here in it's entirety, followed by **istarian**'s response:

> ## Video games: any estimation of the production cost for a boxed edition as a reward?
>  Hey guys, I'd like to start a Kickstarter campaign in the next months for my PC game. I want to include a boxed edition of my game (with leaflet, mini poster, game burned on DVD) as a reward, but I can't find a good estimation of the production cost for that. Do you have any idea about that? I'm aiming 500 to 800 quantity. Any (cheap!) company (who does DVD burning + packaging) to recommend? Thanks a lot for your help!  

**- felep22**

>  You'll need to carefully consider the expectations of your backers and how well you'll be able to meet them. If you tell them that you'll be giving them a boxed copy as a reward, they'll expect (especially these days) that it will be suitable as a decorative item for display on a shelf and be durable enough to survive shipping, occasionally handling and showing off, and personal moves (new home, new state, etc).
> 
> Additionally, you're going to have to figure how many of the boxed editions you need/anticipate needing and communicate with companies that do bulk manufacturing to figure out exactly what it's going to cost you. Expect to get more product than you'll actually need (i.e. number of orders) because there may be some defective products in the batch and some shipments might get lost in the mail. In addition, it's not usually difficult to dispose of extras if the kickstarter is successful (raffles, prizes, etc).
> 
> That said, you'll need some pretty nice art for all of it. You may want to have something created (new or compiled) specifically for the respective parts: leaflet, poster, box, cd. The box should be made of a nice stiff material (similar to heavier cardstock) with a glossy finish at least on the outside. Also most people will probably expect there to be some form of art on the disc itself (just writing the name on it with a sharpie won't cut it), so you probably want silk-screened discs. You might to able to get leaflets/posters done by a local print shop if there is one near you. Costs will probably vary a fair bit and be affected by bulk.
> 
> Boxes:  
> <http://www.printmybox.com/>  
> <http://www.boxcoop.com/>  
> 
> Posters/pamphlets:  
> <http://www.vistaprint.com/>  
> <https://www.upsstoreprint.com/all-products>  
> 
> As for discs, you'll want to find a company that can do silk screened disks and maybe provide cases too. That is, you just have the disc done by them and you put the package together yourself (box, leaflet, mini-poster, DVD). However, some companies will provide blank discs that you'll have to burn and others may be able to burn them for you. In either case, you'll want to produce a master disc image (.iso, .img) with the correct files, folder structure, installer, readme/install instructions, license information, etc.
> 
> Printed Discs:  
> <http://www.mediaxpress.net/custom-printed-blank-CD.html>  
> <http://www.discmakers.com/products/OnDiscPrinting.asp>  
> 
> A quick trip through the latter's price estimation makes it out to be $1000-$1500 for a package deal where they replicate the disc and provide packaging (est. includes shipping). That seems like the low end of costs, at least if you want DVDs. So, $2-$3 per disc+case. Certainly go run the numbers yourself. Based on games in cheap packaging sold at some retail stores (I used to have a copy of Civ III that way) being ~$5 that sounds about right. Might be less in greater bulk.
> 
> Apparently replication and duplication are somewhat different processes with the former being a better, and hence more expensive product (the estimate I checked above included replication). Trying to be super cheap is probably not a good idea here.
> 
> I know nothing about these companies, having not done anything like this or dealt with them myself So, caveat emptor with regard to online businesses. Still, they are advertising services and costs and many things are done over the internet (and occasionally phone) these days and you probably won't have a local 'brick and mortar' store that provides these services.
> 
> Ultimately costs will vary depending on bulk, the company you use, when you want it, etc. When you think about buying boxed games in the past, probably at least 25-50% of the cost was materials and art production, etc. I would bet they banked on making enough sales that a few dollars of profit each would be well worth it.
