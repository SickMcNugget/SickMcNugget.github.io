# What's Up?
Today I decided it was worth some introspection. 
I've been feeling a sense of melancholy and even dread over the last few months.
Increasingly, I feel like I'm wasting my time and that I should be doing bigger and better things with my life.
For reference, I'm in my mid-20s, in case those feelings ring true with the one person who may read this one day.

I know, of course, that I'm not actually wasting 100% of my time.
Sometimes I read, sometimes I practice playing the piano, sometimes I do some 3D modelling or music production or even work on making video games.
All this stuff is really great, but that sense of dread hasn't abated, even with consistent exercise and trying to ground myself in the reality of my life in the present.
I've been increasingly feeling that these emotions are actually the result of the way I interact with people.

# Into the Limbic System
I feel that most of my conversations with people go the same way.
I'll say something earnest or express interest in a topic that I have some passion about and the immediate response that I'll receive from others is: 
1. an expression of disinterest
2. a remark about my nature as a person ("you WOULD be into that")
3. the assumption that I was actually making a joke instead of being serious.

Now for point 3, I have cultivated a personality of making jokes most of the time so I do understand why that reaction is so common. In fact, that one bothers me least of the three.
What does bother me is remarks over who I am.
These remarks aren't usually framed with any positivity whatsoever.
If someone said, "everyone's different, I guess," I wouldn't have a problem.
Unfortunately, most comments are more abrasive than that and I find they stay in my mind for the remainder of the day.

The question is, why don't I let people know that these comments affect me so much.
It's not like they're my mortal enemies or anything, they're my friends and family.
I think the answer lies in my conflict-avoidant nature. 
More specifically, I used to be fine with conflict. If someone said made a snide remark, I would calmly let them know that I don't appreciate that behaviour and then move on.
Time is my greatest enemy in this situation. I've had many arguments where I know exactly where they're going from the beginning and yet I feel powerless to stop them going there.

Over a long enough amount of time, the human brain starts to optimise/learn how to effectively handle these situations.
My brain learned that hiding away/saying as little as possible would eliminate all these conflicts instead of having history repeat itself ad nauseam.
Now I find myself in the situation where I don't really talk about anything of substance to anybody.
All that does to the mind is isolate it, which is one of the worst outcomes for creatures as social as humans.

I'm more disappointed than anything else in my inability to speak openly with my friends and family about my problems.
I'd love to be able to share how I'm feeling with them without it feeling like a mistake later on.
Something needs to change and so I'm going to be implementing some new approaches to life going forward.

## Aside: Neck Pain Retraining
Since mid-August in 2024, I've had chronic neck pain.
For the first year of this pain, I was barely able to function normally at all.
I'd wake up in pain, go to work, sit in pain all day with a whole lot of brain fog and then come home and lie down on my bed for a nap, hoping that sleep would help. This cycle continued until November 2025.

I started going to the gym consistently with my dad. 
At first, I was going 5 days a week for about a month.
After that, I decided to drop down to 3 days a week since it was more sustainable for me than 5 days.
The more I went to the gym, the more my pain started going away, but very slowly.
I assumed that going to the gym was repairing some kind of muscle imbalance that was leading to my neck pain.

One day at work this year, I felt so much neck pain again that it caused me to faint. 
For a couple of weeks following that fainting episode, my pain had returned nearly to its original level in 2024.
I couldn't believe it. I'd done all this work to improve my strength and address some of the underlying mechanical problems that were likely to be causing the pain (supported by the multiple doctors and experts I've seen), yet it had all come back in the span of one day.

I don't know how I came across it, but I found this YouTube page called This Might Hurt, which has a documentary associated with it by the same name.
I haven't seen the documentary itself since it's behind a paywall, but some of the resources relating to this documentary, such as a YouTube video with Dr Howard Schubiner, have helped me.
For reference, there is a video regarding [knowing if your symptoms are neuroplastic](https://youtu.be/ic6se1fOpkc?si=E7zx7kQkU20vOUTT) and another one regarding [Pain Reprocessing Therapy](https://youtu.be/FVDR7sM3WxY?si=MbCp9pjWNhrrk4Z9).
The first video especially made something click in my brain. Whilst I still believe that some parts of my pain are related to physical characteristics of my body (and past injuries), I also believe that a great deal of my pain is neuroplastic.

This simple "click" moment can make a huge difference. I've personally seen a significant reduction in the amount of pain I feel day-by-day.
Writing about this right now I'm feeling more pain than I usually do, presumably because I'm thinking about past pain responses and emotions that cause my symptoms to flare up.
Recognising where the pain is, acknowledging its existence and then moving on seems to be a strong enough approach to significantly reduce the pain response produced by the body, as long as you truly believe it's neuroplastic in nature.
All this pain reprocessing leads me to ask the question: could I use the same recognition technique to retrain my emotional response to situations?

## Making Changes
With some new techniques under my belt, I'd like to strengthen my resolve and move towards a version of myself that I'm more happy with emotionally.
Here's what I'm going to try:
1. When someone remarks on my nature as a person in a way that makes me feel smaller, I'm going to instantly assert that I don't appreciate the way they've said that. I won't insult them or belittle them, I'll just inform them.
2. Later in the day, I'll revisit the conversation, the effect of their words on my emotions and their exact response to my assertion.
3. I will not think any further about the conversation.

I'm hoping these three steps will help retrain the way I approach situations more broadly.
I'd like to act when I feel the impulse to do so instead of doing nothing.
I believe that in the long term, these changes will not just help when I'm talking with people, but they'll also allow me to leave my comfort zone more often and get out of my head more often.
I think that sometimes, one small change can have rippling effects on the other aspects of one's life that they won't recognise until well into the future.
